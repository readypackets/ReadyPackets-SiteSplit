# ReadyPackets Campaign Script: “The Business I Could Finally Explain”

**Format:** 60-second brand film with audio-only adaptation  
**Campaign purpose:** Introduce ReadyPackets through a credible, human story about moving from scattered ideas to organized business materials.  
**Status:** **Fictionalized composite.** This is a dramatized marketing concept, not a testimonial from a real ReadyPackets customer.

> **Required use label:** “Dramatized customer story. Individual circumstances and results vary.”

## Creative premise

Maya Thompson is a fictional founder of a growing local services business. She is capable, committed, and already doing real work for clients, but her business information lives across notes, spreadsheets, email threads, and unfinished documents. When a prospective partner asks clear questions about her plan, operations, and project scope, she realizes that the problem is not her ambition. It is that she cannot yet **show the business clearly**.

ReadyPackets helps Maya create a structured foundation: a business plan, operations framework, and execution materials delivered through one organized customer portal. The story deliberately avoids promises of funding, revenue, legal protection, or business outcomes. Its emotional payoff is confidence, clarity, and readiness to have the next conversation.

## Audience and tone

| Element | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary audience** | Founders, independent operators, consultants, contractors, and early-stage business owners who have a viable idea or active business but lack organized, professional materials. |
| **Emotional arc** | Overwhelmed and uncertain → focused and supported → prepared and confident. |
| **Voice** | Warm, intelligent, composed, and practical. Avoid hype, urgency tactics, or claims that sound too good to be true. |
| **Visual world** | Real workspaces, notebooks, a modest office or home office, purposeful closeups of hands organizing materials, and a clean ReadyPackets portal experience. Navy, white, and restrained gold accents should carry the brand. |
| **Music** | Minimal piano and warm, restrained percussion. Start sparse; build slightly as Maya gains clarity; end cleanly without a dramatic “success” flourish. |

## Production safeguards and approval checklist

1. **Keep the fictional label visible.** Add “Dramatized customer story” in the video description, landing-page copy, and final end-card legal line. If the video uses an actor, do not present the actor by name as a customer.
2. **Do not use real customer material.** Record all portal scenes in a seeded demo environment or as designed motion graphics. Blur-free footage of a real customer portal, order, file, invoice, email, calendar, or admin dashboard must not be used.
3. **Do not imply guaranteed outcomes.** Do not show a funded bank account, signed investment, “approved” stamp, financial gain, legal filing acceptance, or client contract award. The story is about organization and readiness—not a promised commercial result.
4. **Use accurate product wording.** If specific packet names appear, use current names and approved descriptions from the live ReadyPackets catalogue. Avoid implying legal, investment, tax, accounting, or other licensed professional advice where it is not provided.
5. **Confirm the current call to action.** Before publishing, verify the correct public website address, landing page, contact path, product visibility settings, and any local advertising-disclosure requirements.
6. **Capture accessibility assets.** Produce an accurate caption file, a full transcript, and descriptive audio where the visual story contains information not spoken in the ad.

## Campaign positioning guardrail

This campaign should make prospective customers feel understood—not pressured. Its central promise is deliberately modest and credible:

> **ReadyPackets helps turn a business vision into organized materials for clearer next-step conversations.**

Do not replace this with claims such as “get funded,” “guaranteed approval,” “legal protection,” “instant growth,” or “we build your entire business for you.” Those claims would make the message less believable and could create avoidable compliance, reputational, and customer-expectation risk.
